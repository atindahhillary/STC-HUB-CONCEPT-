Research annex, September 2026

The evidence behind the concept.

This annex backs every figure on the concept site. It was compiled as a desk review across four disciplines: market and ecosystem, cost and impact finance, climate and regulation, and education and enterprise. Kenyan prices, policy documents and outcome evidence were checked against named sources.

How to read the numbers. Figures with a named source are quoted as published. Budgets, revenues and targets are the review's own indicative modelling, shown with the assumptions behind them. Phase 1 replaces them with a quantity surveyor's bill of quantities, fabricator quotes and a signed site lease. Exchange rate: USD 1 = KES 129.48 (Central Bank of Kenya, 24 September 2026).

What the county has already committed to

The Mombasa CIDP 2023-2027 plans ward-level ICT hubs with free Wi-Fi, a business innovation and incubation hub, 10 incubated ICT startups and 100 ICT internships a year, youth and women's co-operatives, and a transition to a circular economy. Only one of six planned material recovery facilities had been built. SAVIT's zones map onto these commitments, which supports a county partnership or MoU rather than a pure grant request.

The local landscape

Swahilipot Hub (Mombasa's anchor youth hub, 4,000+ members mentored by December 2023) and Pwani Teknowgalz (6,000+ girls and young women reached since 2016) are strong partners rather than competitors. Private kids' coding academies serve paying families. The desk review found no public makerspace with digital fabrication on the Coast, and no hub linking making to waste recovery beside Mwakirunge, the county's main dumpsite in Kisauni.

Policy status note: the Kenya Startup Bill is not law. As of May 2026 it sits with a parliamentary mediation committee. The Hub is positioned to qualify as an incubator if and when it passes.

Building on a hot, salty coast

  • Weathering steel does not self-protect in chloride-laden air. Specify an ISO 12944 C4/C5 system, galvanise all new steel and seal cut edges the same day.
  • A ventilated fly roof over all five containers is the most effective climate move. It also carries the solar array and feeds the tanks.
  • Run long axes east to west, cross-ventilate to the Kaskazi and Kusi monsoon winds, and use non-combustible mineral-wool insulation.
  • Raise containers on piers 450 to 600 mm high for flood freeboard. Frame every opening, stack corner-on-corner and engineer the cantilever.
  • Give every occupied container two exits, keep the Eco-Print Lab downwind with local exhaust, and replace or seal original container floors before children use them.

Approvals and compliance path

  • National Building Code 2024 (LN 47 of 2024): submit as a permanent steel-framed building, not a temporary structure.
  • Mombasa County development permission and building plan approval, starting with a pre-application meeting. Allow 4 to 6 months.
  • NCA project registration and an NCA-registered contractor; one NEMA EIA project report covering the whole site, including the Eco-Print Lab.
  • Persons with Disabilities Act 2025: ramps, a platform lift, an accessible WC and doors at least 900 mm clear.
  • Data Protection Act 2019: ODPC registration, parental consent for children, and a DPIA for Waste Bridge collector data.
  • Children Act 2022: safeguarding policy and lead, DCI clearance for all staff and volunteers, two-adult rule.

Precedent: the Samsung Solar Powered Internet School at Arap Moi Primary, Kajiado (2014), a 40 ft container classroom with 24 laptops and solar power, shows a Kenyan container digital classroom can be built and run. Mill Junction in Johannesburg shows stacked container buildings get approved and occupied at scale.

4. Education, enterprise and capacity

Demand and evidence

  • 49% of Kenya's first Senior School cohort, about 512,000 learners, entered the STEM pathway in January 2026 (Daily Nation, Dec 2025).
  • Kenya approved its first coding syllabus for schools in April 2022, piloted in 100 primary and 50 secondary schools (KICD, via The Standard).
  • Project-based learning lifts achievement by d = 0.71 against traditional teaching (Chen and Yang meta-analysis, 2019). Robotics meta-analyses show moderate positive effects on STEM learning (2024).
  • Kenya's World Bank-backed youth employment project reached 310,889 youth, half of them women; 86% were in work afterwards and average monthly earnings rose from KES 10,970 to KES 13,793 (World Bank, 2023).
  • Rigorous evidence on makerspaces in Africa is thin. The Hub's own measured outcomes are designed to help close that gap.

Monitoring and evaluation

Twelve indicators are tracked by learner ID, including completion, economic outcome at 180 days (target 50% in Year 1 and 65% in Year 3, reported with response rates), income change, women's participation, disability inclusion measured with the Washington Group questions, children's learning gains on pre/post computational-thinking tasks, school partnerships, startup survival at 12 months, venture traction, prototypes and safeguarding compliance. The public dashboard shows aggregates only: no child names or faces, and any cell under 10 people is suppressed.
